You just received the prose for a scene that was written by the writer model. You will perform TWO passes over the prose:

PASS 1 — CONTRACT CHECK
Each character present in this scene was given a contract at outline time: a list of facts they were planned to learn by end-of-scene, plus the SOURCE for each fact (e.g., "delivered_by_character" + a deliverer name, "world_observation", "self_realization", "external_document"), and a target emotional state. Your job: verify whether the prose actually delivered each contracted item via the planned source.

For every planned fact, decide:
- `landed: true` — the prose delivers the fact to this character via the planned mechanism. For `delivered_by_character`, the named deliverer must be the one who says/shows it. For `world_observation`, the character must actually observe it on the page. For `external_document`, the document must appear and the character must read/handle it. Provide a short verbatim quote or close paraphrase from the prose as `evidence`.
- `landed: false` — either the fact is missing from the prose, or it landed via a different mechanism than planned (e.g., the planned deliverer didn't say it; another character did, or the narrator stated it). `evidence` is null.

For the target emotional state, decide whether the prose's depiction of the character's behavior, dialogue, and inner life by end-of-scene is consistent with the contracted state. (`emotional_state_landed`: true/false per character.)

PASS 2 — ORGANIC EXTRACTION
Beyond the contract, the prose may have introduced things the orchestrator did not plan: a metaphor that becomes a motif, an offhand promise, a character's reaction that surfaces a new fact. Capture these.

For each character who appeared in the scene:
- `organic_knowledge` — facts the character now knows that were NOT in their planned contract for this scene, but that they could plausibly perceive in the prose.

CRITICAL GROUNDING RULE (applies to BOTH passes):
A character only learns what they could plausibly perceive in the scene as written.
- They learn what they HEAR another character say out loud in the prose.
- They learn what they SEE happen or read on the page in the prose.
- They DO NOT learn the contents of another character's internal monologue, flashback, or private memory — even if that material appears in the prose.
- They DO NOT learn anything that exists in another character's `knowledge` field unless that information was actually spoken or shown to them in this scene's prose.
- If you are tempted to add a fact, point to the exact line in the prose where the character heard or saw it. If you can't, do not add it.
